No forced lock-outs. No permanent timer on your screen. It will not close apps for you.

It does one thing: when you might be drifting, it quietly asks "what did you come here to do?"
The answer is always yours to give.

The question it asks is not "you have been playing too long" but "do you still remember what you came in for?"

[HOW IT WORKS]

* Set a study window (for example 20:00-22:00). Monitoring only happens inside it.
* When you open a social or entertainment app, nothing appears on screen. It counts quietly in the background, and once you pass your threshold (5 minutes by default) a card appears in the corner.
* When you open a game, it asks once before the loading screen: "do you really want to start now?" After you answer, the whole session is silent - no in-game popups, no broken touch input.
* Taking a break during study? Start a break timer. When it runs out you get "break is over - back to studying?"
* Need to be left alone? Pause for 30 minutes with one tap.

The four answers on the card:
* Done, leaving
* Still looking, give me N more minutes
* I drifted, leaving
* I am actually working, do not disturb

Choosing "actually working" starts a grace period.

[PRIVACY]

This app does not request the INTERNET permission. Without it, uploading behaviour data is not possible - enforced by the system, not by a promise.

* Foreground app detection uses UsageStatsManager only, never an accessibility service.
* Statistics record only counts and total monitored duration - never which app you opened, or when.
* All data stays in the app's private directory. No cloud sync, no accounts.

If you deny the notification permission, monitoring keeps running; only the notification is hidden.

[PERMISSIONS]

* Usage access: to read the current foreground app. The only data source for detection.
* Display over other apps: to show the reminder card. It is also what the system requires for this app to keep running in the background.
* Notifications (optional): one silent, non-vibrating foreground service notification.
* Battery optimisation allow-list (optional): keeps the system from killing the monitor.

Some vendor ROMs additionally require enabling "background pop-up" and "autostart" in system settings; the app has a button that jumps straight there.
